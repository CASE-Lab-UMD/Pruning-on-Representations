Demystifying When Pruning Works via Representation Hierarchies

Why does neural network pruning preserve standard classification and fixed-target scores, yet trigger catastrophic collapse in autoregressive generation? We provide a comprehensive representation-space answer across hidden states (h), logits (z), and probabilities (p).

Core Scientific Insights

Why Pruning Exhibits Task-Dependent Behavior

Unveiling the fundamental mechanisms that separate non-generative benchmark robustness from autoregressive decoding instability.

1. The Representation Triad

Hidden state embeddings (h in R^d) capture semantic direction, logits (z = Wh) determine candidate score margins, and probabilities (p = softmax(z/T)) drive the final multinomial sampling.

cos(h) → cos(z) → KL(p' || p)

2. Orthogonal Perturbation Rotations

Decomposing perturbation into parallel scaling and orthogonal rotation proves that parallel scaling merely scales confidence, whereas orthogonal rotation alters the ranking of candidate tokens.

1 - cos(h) ≈ 0.5 · (||Δh_⊥|| / ||h||)²

3. Subspace Shield vs. Decoding Drift

Answer-option subspaces in MCQ tasks ({A,B,C,D}) remain structurally separated under pruning. In contrast, multi-step autoregressive generation accumulates probability drift, cascading into trajectory collapse.

MCQ Margin: Robust | Auto-Reg PPL: Drift

Empirical Verification

Deep Dive into Empirical Phenomena

Key figures illustrating why representation hierarchies solve the pruning paradox.

Non-Generative Benchmark Stability

In single-step evaluations (MMLU, ARC, fixed GSM8K), models evaluate relative score differences over fixed context. Because the answer-option subspace preserves relative ranking, accuracy remains high even when large portions of weights or layers are removed.

Figure 3: MMLU / ARC resilience under 20-50% pruning

Generative Quality Breakdown

In multi-step autoregressive generation (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval, HumanEval), every token chosen feeds back into the prompt for the next step. Minor probability shifts cause token substitutions that rapidly steer the generation into catastrophic drift or collapse.

Figure 4: Autoregressive degradation curves across pruning ratios

Attention Sublayer Sensitivity

Attention sublayers exhibit sharp, localized sensitivity to pruning in early-to-middle layers. Perturbations in query-key routing propagate dramatically into downstream probability entropy.

Figure 2 (Left): Attention representation cosine similarity across depth

Option Subspace vs Global Vocabulary

Restricting logits to the multiple-choice option subspace {A, B, C, D} shields predictions from global vocabulary-level noise, directly explaining the survival of non-generative benchmark scores.

Figure 7: Subspace logit margin invariance under pruning

Local Representation Deviation

For any representation vector x, the cosine deviation induced by pruning is strictly governed by the ratio of orthogonal perturbation magnitude:

"Parallel perturbations rescale representation magnitude without altering angular direction; orthogonal perturbations rotate candidate rankings."

Theorem 2 Logit → Probability

Probability Space Sensitivity

Rewriting probability-space cosine deviation in terms of logit perturbation Δz, scaled by the temperature factor 1 / (2 T²):

"Quantifying how probability cosine sensitivity scales inversely with the square of softmax temperature."

Distributional KL Divergence
